twin interviewer

A good listener that learns how someone talks.

An AI chats with a person, reacts like a friend, asks one easy question at a time, and collects two things: what they know, and how they say it. Later that becomes their digital twin.

Design v4Built and tested with real keysNew: simulated interviews

How it works

1

Someone asks for a twin

"My mother, so I can ask her recipes at night."

2

The AI interviews them

A short, real reaction, then one question. The person does most of the talking.

3

Two jars fill up

What they know, and how they talk: pet phrases like "like…" or "correct?", fillers, mixed languages.

4

A twin is built

Built later from the jars. This project is the interviewer, plus a rough twin to test it.

Every answer goes two ways

The fast lane keeps the chat moving. The slow lane thinks about what to ask next.

FastSave the answer and reply~5 s text · instant voice
SlowTake notes, read the mood, pick a gentle tip for the next turn~6 s

The person never waits for the thinking. Times are measured with real OpenAI calls; each AI role gets its own reasoning effort, which cut replies from 10–16 s to about 5 s. If the tab closes before the slow lane runs, a clean-up job catches up later.

Reading the room

The person is a volunteer, so their interest comes before any checklist.

Lit upLet them run.
FlatAsk a different kind of question, or offer a choice.
Warms up againnewFollow them at once. One clearly engaged answer ends a flat stretch.
Avoiding a topicDrop it and never bring it back. Short or tired answers don't count as avoiding.
Goes quietnewAfter 7 s (or 15 s if a question is waiting), offer an easier question or a choice. At most twice.
TiredWrap up on a high note.
Can't tellCarry on as normal.

Slowing down needs a trend over 2–3 answers, measured against the person's own normal. Warming up counts straight away, because nagging someone who has opened up costs more than a wrong guess.

New in v4

Catching how they talk

Facts are easy to collect. What makes a twin sound like the person is their verbal habits: the word they lean on, the tag they add, the phrase they always reach for.

They say

"Savings first, spending after, correct?"

An LLM spots

After each chat it reads everything they've said and suggests habits, with how they use them.

A count checks

A habit is kept only if it really repeats: at least 3 times, in at least 2 answers. Nothing invented survives.

Real examples too

The same step picks 4–5 real question → answer pairs that best show their voice, skipping tired or cut-off moments.

The twin continues them

It sees those pairs as earlier turns of its own chat, plus how long their answers usually are. The interviewer never sees any of it, so it never copies their slang.

Showing beats telling. With rules alone, the twin wrote 150-word answers for people who talk in 20-word ones, and the habit got lost.

The AI team

The thinking is split into small roles, each with one job and its own prompt. Only one of them ever talks to the person.

Before any of this, the brief compiler turns the creator's request into a TwinSpec (who, why, which topics, what never to raise). Every role reads it.

Moods that react new

A timed mood only tests whether the interviewer notices how someone feels. Now the persona's mood is earned: after every interviewer turn, a judge playing that person scores it from −2 (dulled them) to +2 (lit them up). Their energy, 0 to 10, sets their mood. Late in the call every turn costs a little energy, so only good questions keep them going.

2 → 10Venkat's energy in the first two minutes: bored to excited, from concrete follow-ups on his first day at the bankengages well
−2"What part of you softens first?": the one feelings question dropped him hardestwatch
2½ minof "Thank you, Uncle" back and forth after the goodbye; nothing hangs up the callfound a bug
WorkingThe avoided topic was never raised in any run. Facts a persona mentioned reached the notes 25 times out of 28. Every after-call pipeline finished. Wrap-ups now land in the tired phase or at the target time.
Fixed between roundsShort warm replies ("Haha, that makes me so happy") no longer count as flat, and only a solid flat run ends a chat early. "I'd like to rest" no longer becomes a banned topic. After-call jobs retry.
Just adjusted, not yet re-runThe analysis no longer holds the chat lock during its AI call, so replies never wait behind it. Some reactions played the person's words back ("Same cup, same people."), 7 times with Venkat. The rule now asks for the interviewer's own words. The interviewer slipped into Venkat's "Correct." once.
Still openExcitement often reads as "can't tell" in a first chat, before the person's own normal is known. Deepgram mishears names. Twins under-use very frequent fillers (Kavya's "like" at a third of her rate).

Personas are models, more fluent and cooperative than people, so this is a regression test, not proof of quality with real people. Each run keeps a stereo recording (persona left, interviewer right) and a scorecard.

What changed

Faster thinkingEach AI role gets its own reasoning effort. Text replies went from 10–16 s to about 5 s.
Says hello onceThe interviewer introduces itself in the first message only.
Fewer false "avoiding""I don't know" or a tired short answer no longer bans a topic forever.
Transcripts per languageDeepgram settings follow the person's languages: English adds sentiment, Hinglish uses multi-language mode.
Interruptions keptThe interviewer's own echo is removed from the tapes, but a person cutting in is kept.
Reliable greetingGPT-Live gets a start nudge, so the call never opens in silence.
Reacts like a personA short real reaction, then one question, and never a "Thank you" with nothing to answer.
Quiet rescueIf nobody speaks for 7–15 s, the interviewer gently offers an easier way in.
Quick recoveryOne engaged answer ends a flat stretch.
Moods aren't facts"I'm very tired today" is no longer saved as a fact, so the twin doesn't copy one moment's mood.
Speech habitsPet phrases are captured with a rate and handed to the twin only.
Shown, not toldThe twin gets real question → answer pairs as earlier chat turns, plus their usual answer length. Kavya's "like" went from 3.7 to 6.8 per 100 words (she says 14.4).

Still open: Deepgram mishears place names ("Lumding" became "Longding"), and the twin can pick those up. Next step is to pass names the live transcript heard as hints.

Faster repliesText replies no longer wait for the analysis.
Playable tapesEach audio piece is a full one-minute file, overlapping by 2 s.
Safe numberingTurn order and duplicate checks moved from Redis to Postgres.
No loose endsClosed tabs and idle chats are ended and processed automatically.
One clockEvery timestamp counts from when the mic turns on.
Cleaner dataA notes table, smaller snapshots, a separate baseline per measurement.

Build order

Text first, because it's cheap and easy to debug. Voice once the brain works.

  1. Setup + transcription test done
  2. Text chat done
  3. Measure coverage done
  4. Read the mood done
  5. Voice done, real keys
  6. Test the twin twin + simulator; A/B judge next
  7. More people not started
Runs on free tiers (Vercel, Supabase, Upstash). The only real costs are OpenAI and Deepgram after its free credit. An 8-minute simulated interview costs about $1.
