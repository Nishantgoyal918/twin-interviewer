Twin Interviewer · AI design · v2

An interviewer that listens while it talks

A voice interviewer that collects content (what someone knows) and style (how they say it) to build a digital twin of them, while keeping a volunteer engaged.

02One turn, two lanes

The reply never waits for the thinking.

sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  participant P as Person
  participant G as GPT-Live
  participant B as Browser
  participant F as /turn (fast)
  participant S as /analyze (slow)
  P->>G: speaks
  G->>P: replies at once (its own timing)
  G-->>B: transcript deltas
  B->>F: interviewer turn + their turn + audio features
  F-->>B: saved (idx assigned)
  B->>S: analyze in background
  Note over S: observer LLM (~6 s), tags, planner
  S-->>B: decision + tip
  B->>G: session.thinking.append(tip)
  Note over G: uses it on a later turn
Consistency rules
  • /turn holds a per-session Redis lock (waits ≤ 5 s, else 409); idx comes from Postgres; idempotent on client_turn_id.
  • /analyze locks twice: read → unlocked observer call → apply. It re-checks analyzed_at, so two analyses of one turn stay idempotent.
  • SessionState lives in Redis (hot) with a Postgres snapshot per decision; last_analyzed_idx never moves backwards.
  • Backchannels from the person (≤ 2 words, < 1 s) aren't posted as turns.
  • Turns the browser never analyzed are picked up at session end (analyze_backlog).
Observer output (one LLM call per answer)
FieldRule
facts[]durable facts from the latest answer only; kind = fact | preference | person | story_hook | followup. Passing states ("tired today") are never facts.
topics[]1–4 labels
registerinstructing | storytelling | opinion | reacting | small_talk | other
deflectiontrue only on an explicit refusal of a topic. Vague or tired answers are not deflection (it bars the topic forever).
lexical_engagementhigh | medium | low | unclear, from the words only. A short reply to a remark, or a cut-off fragment → unclear. Laughter is never low.
slot_updates[]which coverage slots this answer filled (partial | done)

It replaces a separate tone-judge call: one LLM call per turn, not two.

  • Every feature becomes a z-score against this person's baseline (Welford running stats, per twin × modality × source). Only |z| ≥ 1 makes a tag.
  • Confidence = min(0.9, 0.3 + n/50); acted on only at ≥ 0.5.
  • Cold start (baseline under 20 turns): a within-session baseline, confidence capped at 0.4, so early calls run on words alone.
  • Never emits emotion labels as facts.
Goal choice
  • Goals come from the twin spec: content domains (high → low priority) × slots, plus style registers (instructing, storytelling, opinion…).
  • Order: domains with nothing learned yet → partially filled slots → the rest. Goals skipped this session are excluded.
  • Second half of the call, with the goal's whole area untouched → a firm tip: "With your next question, move on: …"
  • Every move-on tip asks for an easy story opener (a favourite, a time when, a proudest moment), not a how-does-it-work question.

How it changes the planner
  • Each video tag adds ±0.5 to the turn score, like audio tags. Thresholds (engaged ≥ 2, flat ≤ −1) and the 2-of-3 trend rule stay as they are.
  • Listening features attach to the previous interviewer turn: a per-question "landed / didn't" signal, so the planner can prefer question kinds that land for this person.
  • Baselines stay per twin × modality × source (video is a new source). Some people never smile on camera: only deviation from their own normal counts.
  • Cold start: within-session baseline, confidence capped at 0.4, as for audio.
  • Still no emotion labels saved as facts, and nothing like "you look tired" said to the person; the signals only steer.
Privacy and cost
  • Separate consent toggle; the person sees their own preview and can turn it off mid-call.
  • No frames recorded, uploaded or sent to GPT-Live: only per-turn aggregates go with /turn.
  • The model is a few MB, cached by the browser; inference runs on the person's device, so $0 server cost.
  • Low-end phones: drop to ~5 fps, or skip video if a frame takes over 100 ms.
Why not send video to a cloud model
  • Latency: frames → vision LLM takes seconds; the planner already runs a few seconds behind.
  • Cost and privacy: a face stream to a third party on every call.
  • Labels aren't what we need: "happy / sad" from one frame is unreliable; trends against the person's own baseline are.
How we'd know it helps
  • The simulator has no face, so this needs real calls: ~20 opt-in sessions, labelled turn by turn (the person's quick rating after the call, plus a reviewer).
  • Replay the same turns through the planner with and without video tags; compare mood-read accuracy (today 10–30%) and topic coverage.
  • Turn it on for everyone only if it clearly beats audio + words; otherwise keep it an opt-in experiment.

04Interviewer rules

Goal: the person talks ≥ 75% of the time, and their style isn't contaminated by ours.

The 12 rules in the system prompt (condensed)
  1. Be a person: a short real reaction, then one question. 2–3 sentences. React in your own words, not theirs.
  2. One question per turn; no multi-part questions. End every turn with something they can answer (unless saying goodbye).
  3. Follow-ups pick one concrete detail ("the blue bicycle" → "Where did you ride it?"). Never generic follow-ups, never quoting them back.
  4. Don't praise every answer. Don't summarize.
  5. Match their formality; never copy their vocabulary, fillers, slang or catchphrases.
  6. Allow silences. Never interrupt a story, even an off-plan one.
  7. Use earlier-session details naturally.
  8. Deflection → back off at once; never return.
  9. One warm goodbye turn when told to wrap up; at most "Bye, take care!" after. Never say goodbye before being told.
  10. Languages: follow the person (Hinglish allowed).
  11. Coach's tips: soft goals can wait; "move on" tips happen at the end of their current thought, with one reaction and a bridge.
  12. Never claim to be human.
Browser-side conversation rules
  • Dead air: silence for 7 s after a statement or 15 s after a question → one nudge (an easier question or a choice). Max 2 until they speak. "Silence" = no transcribed words, so fans and TVs don't count.
  • Tips: soft tips are sent once; firm tips are re-sent after any interviewer question that didn't make the move. No tips once the call is ending.
  • Opening: the instructions alone didn't make GPT-Live speak first within 25 s; a commentary nudge after session.started does (2.5–10 s).

05Ending a call

Two polite parties trade "bye" forever, so the browser hangs up, not the model.

stateDiagram-v2

Why each rule exists (all found in simulated calls)
  • "Mm." isn't a farewell: turns of ≤ 2 words don't count.
  • Hang up after the interviewer's reply, not after silence: bye/bye ping-pong never goes quiet.
  • Its own goodbye counts as wrapping: otherwise the dead-air nudge or a coach's tip restarted the call with a fresh question right after "Bye, take care!".
  • Reopen once only: the second farewell ends the call.

Recording and transcription details
  • Mic segments: rotating recorders, 60 s each with 2 s overlap, so each is a complete WebM file (timesliced WebM chunks aren't decodable alone). Presigned PUT straight to storage, with an IndexedDB retry queue.
  • Whole call: a separate stereo recording (interviewer left, person right) in 10-minute parts, for listening back only.
  • Deepgram: nova-3; the language follows the person (en keeps "um", multi handles Hinglish but drops fillers); sentiment for English only. Async with a callback; audio goes by signed URL and never passes through our functions.
  • Alignment: one session clock (t0 = mic start). A word goes to the turn whose [start − 300 ms, end + 300 ms] contains it. Echo removal: the interviewer's words leaking into the mic are matched in order and time and dropped; barge-in words are kept.

How examples and habits are picked
  • Speech habits: a pet phrase counts only if seen ≥ 3 times across ≥ 2 answers; stored with its rate per 100 words.
  • Voice examples: the LLM picks 4–5 pairs that cover their range of styles. Code first drops answers that are tired (low engagement), split mid-sentence, under 15 words, or about a passing mood ("I'm exhausted").
  • Length line: "usually about {median} words, rarely over {p90}". This was the single biggest fix.
  • gpt-5 wasn't worth it: no better on style, much slower, and it invented a fact.

Mood model and scorecard
  • Energy 0–10: ≤ 3 bored (exhausted past stamina), 4–6 smiling, ≥ 7 excited. Each non-backchannel interviewer turn: energy += effect; past stamina, −0.5 per turn.
  • Headline: brief topics covered (a domain counts once a slot is done), facts mentioned vs the hidden card, average energy. A happy call that covers one topic is a failure.
  • Also: turn quality (no echo, one question, under 60 words), talk share, mood-read accuracy, wrap-up timing, sensitive topic never raised, habits captured / used by the twin / not copied by the interviewer, twin probe answers.
