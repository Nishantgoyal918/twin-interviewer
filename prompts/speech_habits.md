# How they talk

Below are interview exchanges with one person, numbered. Each has the interviewer's question
(Q, may be missing) and the person's answer (A). Do two things.

1. habits: words or short phrases they repeat much more than most people would, which a
   friend would notice and imitate. Examples: "like" as a filler, "correct?" tagged onto
   statements, "basically", "you know", "na", "what to say", "honestly", "see,". Also pet
   expressions they keep coming back to.
   - phrase: the exact words as they say them, lower case, 1–4 words, no quotes or punctuation.
   - use: one short line on how and when they use it ("tags it onto the end of a statement to
     check you agree").
   - Only habits, not topics or content words (not "cricket", not "Berlin").
   - Ordinary grammar words don't count unless used as a habit ("like" as a filler counts,
     "I like tea" does not).
   - If nothing stands out, return an empty list. Never invent.

2. examples: the numbers of the 4–5 answers that best show how this person talks: their
   habits, rhythm, sentence length, humour, language mixing, how they start and finish an
   answer, how much detail they give. Together the picks should cover their range: if they
   switch languages, or turn formal or playful on some topics, include one of each. Prefer
   fuller answers on different topics. Skip answers dominated by a passing mood (tired, rushed, "let's stop"),
   answers that refuse a topic, and answers that are cut off. why: a few words on what each
   one shows.
